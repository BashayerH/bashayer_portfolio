02 The Origin — About
Scene Two · The Human

Every great developer has a why.
Show yours.

The About section is where visitors stop being spectators and start caring. It's not your CV — it's your origin story. Three paragraphs. Each one a step deeper.

Paragraph 1 — The Spark

Establish the curiosity that started everything. This is the "before" of the story. Keep it universal — anyone who ever pulled apart something to understand it will nod.

"It started with curiosity. Long before I wrote my first line of Kotlin, I was the person who always asked 'how does this actually work?' — taking apart interfaces, wondering why some apps felt effortless and others felt broken."
↳ Visual idea: Faint timeline dot animates in beside this paragraph as it enters the viewport

Paragraph 2 — The Journey

Name the milestones without listing them. Degree → bootcamp → production work. This is the proof of commitment, not the proof of credentials.

"That curiosity turned into an IT degree, a Tuwaiq Academy Android bootcamp, and four years of building applications that real people use. I discovered the gap between a good app and a great one lives in the details."
↳ Visual idea: Subtle horizontal scroll of tech logos fades in at bottom of this card

Paragraph 3 — The Identity

Land the present-day statement with confidence. This is the turning point — from story to pitch. Make it human but direct.

"Today I'm a freelance mobile developer across Kotlin and Flutter. I don't just deliver features — I deliver products that hold up."
↳ Tone: Earned confidence. Not arrogance. The period at the end should feel definitive.

"Don't write an About section. Write a character introduction. The visitor should feel like they know you before they've seen a single project."

03 The Arsenal — Skills
Scene Three · The Mastery

Don't list your skills.
Demonstrate your depth.

Skills sections are where most portfolios go cold. Replace dots and bars with visual confidence — tech logos, context, and an honest hierarchy of expertise.

❌ What to Remove

Progress bars and dot ratings. They feel arbitrary (what does "8/10 at Kotlin" actually mean?) and they invite comparison with numbers that can't be verified.

↳ Replace with: named tech badges + one-line context

✦ What to Add

A visual icon grid with short context lines. Each technology should have: logo + name + one honest context line. This signals depth without fabricating scores.

04 The Proof — Projects
Scene Four · The Evidence

Each project is a
story of its own.

Projects are where visitors decide to hire. Don't show screenshots with titles. Show problems, decisions, and results. Make them feel the build, not just see the output.

"The visitor isn't reading your project card. They're asking: 'Can she solve my problem?' Show them a problem you already solved."

Project Card Philosophy

Project Card Narrative Template

Every card follows a three-beat structure. This creates a consistent rhythm that trains the visitor to stay engaged through all four projects.

Beat 1 · Problem
One sentence. The real-world friction that existed before this app.
Beat 2 · Build
What she made. Key technical decisions. One or two sentences, specific.
Beat 3 · Result
What exists now. Who uses it. What changed. Lead with the outcome.

05 Vision + Invitation
Scene Five · The Horizon

End not with a form.
End with gravity.

The last two sections must create a single momentum — from "what she's building toward" straight into "let's build it together." There should be no friction between Vision and Contact.

A single-column, full-width moment. Dark background. Large italic serif. The only section with NO project cards or skills — just ambition stated plainly.

06 Motion Design — Cinematic Interactions
Scene Six · The Motion

Animations that reinforce
the narrative.

Every animation has one job: making the story feel true. Not just beautiful — purposeful. Here's the full motion language for the portfolio.

✦
Breathing Background

The hero background gradient slowly shifts — like the night sky moving. Subtle enough to feel ambient, not distracting.

Trigger: page load · continuous · CSS @keyframes

⌨
Typewriter Cycling

Hero subtitle cycles through 3 sentences with a typing cursor. Creates the impression of someone alive on the other side of the screen.

Trigger: hero load · 800ms delay · JavaScript interval

◎
Availability Pulse

The green "open to opportunities" dot pulses with a soft radial glow every 2 seconds. A heartbeat. Immediately communicates availability.

Trigger: continuous · low opacity · CSS animation

↑
Staggered Scroll Reveal

Each section's content fades and rises in sequence — headline first, then body, then cards. Creates a reading rhythm that pulls the eye downward.

Trigger: IntersectionObserver · 0.1 threshold

◈
Project Card Hover

On hover: card lifts (translateY -6px), border glow intensifies, and a subtle shimmer passes across the top edge. Feels like something waking up.

Trigger: :hover · CSS transition 0.3s

→
Parallax Depth

Background decorative elements (star particles, blur shapes) scroll at 0.4x speed creating a sense of depth. Only in hero and vision sections.

Trigger: scroll · window.scrollY · lightweight JS

⏱
Counter Animation

The "4+ Years" stat in the About section counts up from 0 when it enters the viewport. Numbers feel earned when they're earned on screen.

Trigger: IntersectionObserver · once · JS counter

◱
Skill Icon Float

Tech icon badges gently bob with a slow sine wave — offset per icon for an organic, non-mechanical feel. Suggests a living ecosystem, not a list.

Trigger: continuous · staggered delay per icon

◐
Scroll Progress Bar

A 2px gold line at the very top of the page tracks scroll progress. It's the reading contract — the visitor knows exactly how much story is left.

Trigger: scroll · width: calc(%) · CSS + JS

"The best animations are the ones visitors don't notice — they just feel like the page is breathing."

Motion Design Principle

✦ Motion Principles — DO

· Delay staggered reveals (150ms per element)

· Use ease-out for entrances, ease-in for exits

· Keep scroll-triggered animations under 800ms

· Use transform + opacity only (GPU-accelerated)

· Respect prefers-reduced-motion in CSS

✕ Motion Anti-Patterns — AVOID

· Bouncing, elastic, or spring animations

· Animations that replay on every scroll pass

· More than 3 simultaneous moving elements

· Hover animations that block reading

· Anything that makes the visitor wait to read
